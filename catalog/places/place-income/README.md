# Income & Poverty — Places & CDPs

**[🗺️ Open in the data browser](https://browser.portolan-sdi.org/?catalog=https://data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/catalog.json#/external/data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/place-income/collection.json)** · [Download the GeoParquet](https://data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/place-income/place-income.parquet) · [Agent guide](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/place-income/AGENTS.md) · [Source repository](https://github.com/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog)

Income, poverty and employment: median household income and per capita income ([B19013](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B19013), [B19301](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B19301)), the share of people below the poverty line and below twice it ([C17002](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.C17002)), the share of households receiving SNAP ([B22010](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B22010)), the unemployment rate as a share of the civilian labor force ([B23025](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B23025)), the household income distribution collapsed to 7 shares from under $25,000 to $200,000 or more ([B19001](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B19001)), and median earnings for workers with earnings ([B20002](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B20002)) — plus, at every level but block groups, where the Census Bureau does not publish them, the Gini index of income inequality ([B19083](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B19083)) and the poverty rate by age for children under 18 and adults 65 and over ([B17001](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B17001)), plus total population ([B01003](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B01003)) and population density for context.

Curated indicators for all **32,612** incorporated places and census designated places in the United States, Puerto Rico and the island areas, from the U.S. Census Bureau's [American Community Survey](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs.html) **2020–2024 5-year estimates** (`acsdt5y2024`, the table-based [Summary File](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2024/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/)). The Census Bureau calls the ACS "the premier source of detailed information about the nation's people and housing". The Bureau publishes 1-year estimates only for places of 65,000 people or more, so the 5-year release is the only one that covers all 32,612 of them ([areas published](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/geography-acs/areas-published.html)).

Every estimate ships with its margin of error — `<column>_moe`, at the 90% confidence level, the level the Census Bureau publishes — and the headline statistics also carry a coefficient of variation (`<column>_cv` = `moe / 1.645 / estimate`) so small-area noise can be flagged rather than mapped as if certain. Derived percentages propagate error with the Census Bureau's own formulas from [ACS Accuracy of the Data](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/tech_docs/accuracy/MultiyearACSAccuracyofData2024.pdf): sums add in quadrature, proportions use the subset formula with the ratio formula as fallback. ACS jam values for suppressed or unestimable cells are NULL here, never numbers, and medians are published as the Bureau publishes them — a median cannot be aggregated to a larger area. Each column's description names its ACS table, its exact universe, and the lines summed to make it.

Most of the 32,612 rows are small: the file holds every incorporated place and every [census designated place](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/bas/information/cdp.html) the Census Bureau delineates, from New York city to villages of 40 people, so filter on `population` before reading a place-level distribution as if every row were a city. A place is also not a partition of the country: the land between places is not in this file at all, so place rows do not sum to a county or a state. `median_hh_income` is NULL for **12.71%** of places and `per_capita_income` for **4.28%**, and the median coefficient of variation on `median_hh_income` is **0.139** (measured) — **7.3%** of the places that publish one are above the 0.40 "unreliable" line.

Geometries are the Census Bureau's [2024 cartographic boundary files](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/geo/cartographic-boundary.html) for incorporated places and census designated places (GENZ2024, 1:500,000), reprojected to EPSG:4326 and joined on `GEOID`. Cartographic rather than TIGER/Line because TIGER extends census geographies into open water while the cartographic files clip to the shoreline, which is what a choropleth wants. The boundary vintage matches the estimate vintage.

The join is driven by geometry, so a geography with 2024 boundaries but no ACS tabulation keeps its row with NULL values instead of disappearing: 279 places (MP 135, AS 77, GU 57, VI 10) in the island areas, which have cartographic boundaries but no ACS 5-year estimates because the Island Areas run a separate decennial-only program. Two more, Brentwood CDP and Wyandanch CDP, have boundaries and no matching ACS row, both in Suffolk County, New York — the county whose 2024 re-delineation also leaves 14 tracts and 39 block groups without ACS rows elsewhere in this catalog. One more: `2148000` Louisville city, Kentucky, which the boundary file still carries and the ACS tabulates as the consolidated `2148006` Louisville/Jefferson County metro government (balance) instead — so the Louisville you want is `2148006`. Keeping these rows means a national map has no holes and a consumer can see the absence; dropping them would make "every US geography" quietly mean something narrower.

This is one themed chunk of five at this geography. The other themes are [People & Education](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/place-people/collection.json), [Housing & Rent](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/place-housing/collection.json), [Commuting & Connectivity](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/place-connectivity/collection.json) and [Health, Disability & Income by Race](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/place-wellbeing/collection.json), split that way so a consumer who wants one theme reads one file. Chunks at the same geography hold the same rows and the same geometry, so they join 1:1 on `geoid`. A sixth collection, [All Indicators](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/places/collection.json), carries all 135 columns on the same rows in one wide table — at this many features width costs little, so take that one if a question crosses themes. A place nests in a state but not in a county — a place can straddle a county line — so `substr(geoid, 1, 2)` reaches the state row and nothing else joins by prefix. Every other geography in this catalog is organized the same way — [block groups](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/block-groups/catalog.json), [census tracts](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/tracts/catalog.json), [counties](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/catalog.json), [states](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/states/catalog.json), [ZCTAs](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/zctas/catalog.json) and [congressional districts](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/congressional-districts/catalog.json) — and the [blocks](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/blocks/collection.json) collection carries 2020 Decennial counts, a different survey and vintage rather than a finer version of these numbers.

The PMTiles archive is a census-geography pyramid built with [tylertoo](https://github.com/geoparquet-io/tylertoo): z0–z5 are county polygons carrying the Census Bureau's own published county estimates, and z6–z12 are the places themselves. Places do not nest inside counties — a place can cross a county line — so the county band is orientation at national zooms, not a roll-up of these features. Thinning this many polygons down to a national zoom would show a biased subset (small urban geographies drop first) rather than less data, so the coarse zooms step up to a coarser census geography instead of a thinned sample of this one. Every band carries the same column names, so one style expression classifies every zoom — but below z6 you are looking at counties, not places, and the values there are the Bureau's official estimates for those geographies, with their own meaning, rather than a visualization-only average: clicking a coarse feature identifies a real county by `geoid` and `name`. The `*_moe` columns are excluded from the tiles at every zoom — a margin of error is computed for one specific geography and cannot be carried across bands — while the `*_cv` columns are kept. Statistics come from the GeoParquet, never from the tiles.

This is a mirror — [data.census.gov](https://data.census.gov/) is authoritative, and nothing here is an official Census Bureau product. The exact upstream files this collection was built from are published as `source` assets, linked at their original census.gov URLs, so the original is always one click away; the Bureau's own documentation is linked alongside them. Read the collection's [AGENTS.md](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/place-income/AGENTS.md) before querying: it carries the schema, the join keys, the NULL-rate story and tested queries against the published URLs.

## Processing Notes

This collection is a themed cut of [All Indicators — Places & CDPs](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/places/collection.json), the wide 135-column table at this geography: the same rows, the same geometry and the same seven context columns, with 46 columns kept and the other themes' dropped. Nothing is recomputed — every value here is the value in the wide file — so the build notes below describe the single ACS read both were made from.

Built 2026-11-12 from the ACS 2020–2024 5-year table-based Summary File (https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2024/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/), downloaded the same day over plain HTTPS — the summary file needs no API key. Tables read for this collection: B01003, B17001, B19001, B19013, B19083, B19301, B20002, B22010, B23025, C17002. Each national `.dat` file is one row per geography with estimate and margin of error side by side, and every summary level is picked off `GEO_ID` by prefix in a single pass per file — this collection's is `1600000US`, read off the files themselves rather than taken from a summary-level table.

Jam values (the sentinels the ACS publishes in place of unestimable values, e.g. -666666666 for an uncomputable median, and -555555555 for a controlled MOE) become NULL: any estimate or margin at or below -111111111 is dropped rather than published as a number. Counts are sums of the specified lines with margins added in quadrature; percentages use the Census Bureau's proportion formula with the ratio formula as fallback; medians are passed through untouched; coefficients of variation are `moe / 1.645 / estimate` on the headline columns. Every line number is pinned against the release's own table shells before the build starts (44 pins verified, 0 failures, plus 4 derived line groups resolved from the shells), so a vintage bump that moves a line fails loudly instead of publishing a mislabeled column.

Geometry is the 2024 cartographic boundary file (cb_2024_us_place_500k.zip) for incorporated places and census designated places (1:500,000), reprojected to EPSG:4326 with ogr2ogr — not DuckDB's `ST_Transform`, which computes correct coordinates but stamps the source CRS into the GeoParquet metadata. Geometry drives the join, so a geography with boundaries but no ACS tabulation keeps its row with NULL values.

Cross-checked end to end against the published St. Louis census catalog, which runs the same formulas on the same vintage: 314 of 314 block groups joined, 0 mismatches on median household income, its margin of error, the poverty rate and its margin of error — exact equality, not approximate.

Validated: 32,612 rows (the 2024 cartographic boundary file's own feature count; the Bureau's [2020 tallies](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/time-series/geo/tallies.html) give 19,734 incorporated places + 12,454 CDPs = 32,188 for the 2020 vintage), 32,612 distinct `geoid`, no percentage outside 0–100, every one of the 32,330 tabulated ACS places matched to a polygon, 282 polygons with no ACS row kept with NULL values. Spot-checked: Los Angeles city population 3,857,263, New York city 8,483,844.

Converted to GeoParquet with geoparquet-io (gpio): ZSTD compression, Hilbert row order, and a covering `bbox` column with row-group statistics, so a spatial filter can skip most of the file over the network. Tiled to PMTiles with tylertoo as a census-geography pyramid, so coarse zooms carry a coarser census geography — counties, then tracts — with that geography's own official values, instead of a thinned sample of this collection's features; the collections small enough to draw nationally (counties, states, congressional districts) are tiled as real polygons at every zoom.

## Known Issues

Island areas carry geometry but no ACS data. American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and the U.S. Virgin Islands have cartographic boundaries but no ACS 5-year estimates, so their rows publish with NULL values rather than being dropped: 4 states, 13 county equivalents, 126 tracts, 405 block groups, 17 ZCTAs, 279 places and 4 delegate districts.

Places do not tile the country. A place is a settlement — an incorporated city, town, village or borough, or a [census designated place](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/bas/information/cdp.html) standing in for an unincorporated community — so the land between places is absent from this file and place rows do not sum to a county or a state. Places nest in states but not in counties: a place can straddle a county line, which is why there is no county-prefix join.

Most rows are small places. Filter on `population` before reading a place-level distribution as if every row were a city, and expect the suppression rates below.

Three non-island places carry geometry and no ACS row: Brentwood CDP (`3608026`) and Wyandanch CDP (`3683294`) in Suffolk County, New York, and Louisville city, Kentucky (`2148000`), whose ACS tabulation is the consolidated `2148006` Louisville/Jefferson County metro government (balance) — if you want Louisville, use `2148006`.

Most rows are places too small for a published median: NULL for `median_hh_income` on 12.71% of rows (measured). These are the Census Bureau's own suppressions, reported rather than patched.

Margins of error at this geography can be large relative to the estimate. Use the `*_cv` columns to flag or grey out unreliable values; never compare two geographies without checking whether their margins overlap, and never aggregate a median.

PMTiles coarse zooms are a different census geography from this collection's: below z6 you are looking at counties, not places. The values there are the Census Bureau's own estimates for those geographies, not an aggregation of this file, and `*_moe` columns are not in the tiles. Statistics come from the GeoParquet.
