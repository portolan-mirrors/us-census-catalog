# Trends, 2015–2024 — States

**[🗺️ Open in the data browser](https://browser.portolan-sdi.org/?catalog=https://data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/catalog.json#/external/data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/trends/state-trends/collection.json)** · [🧭 Census Atlas](https://portolan-mirrors.github.io/us-census-catalog/#c=trends/state-trends) · [Download the GeoParquet](https://data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/trends/state-trends/state-trends.parquet) · [Agent guide](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/trends/state-trends/AGENTS.md) · [Source repository](https://github.com/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog)

A dozen headline indicators — population, median household income, per capita income, the poverty rate, the unemployment rate, median age, the bachelor's-degree-or-higher share, the share of people of color, households, the owner-occupied share, median home value and median gross rent — carried across two non-overlapping ACS 5-year releases: **2015-2019** (`acsdt5y2019`, the Census Bureau's table-based [prototype summary file](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2019/prototype/5YRData/), validated against the [Census API](https://api.census.gov/data/2019/acs/acs5) before this collection was built — 29 requests, ~4.66 million compared values across every summary level this catalog uses including block groups, 0 mismatches) and **2020-2024** (`acsdt5y2024`, the same release every other ACS collection here reads). **Long form**: one row per `geoid` per `period`, not one row per geoid — a consumer who wants "both years side by side" pivots on `period`, and the `chg_*` columns already carry the 2020-2024-minus-2015-2019 difference on the 2020-2024 row so a map style can classify on it without a join.

All **112** rows at this geography (56 states and state equivalents × 2 periods), in the United States, Puerto Rico and the island areas.

**The two periods do not overlap, which is what makes them comparable.** 2015-2019 and 2020-2024 share no sample year, unlike two 5-year releases one year apart (2019-2023 and 2020-2024, say), which the Census Bureau's own guidance on [Comparing ACS Data](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/guidance/comparing-acs-data.html) says not to compare because their overlapping sample years make a measured difference partly an artifact of shared data. This collection's two periods are five years apart with no shared sample year, so a difference here is a measured change, not a sampling artifact of the comparison itself — though it is still a difference of two margin-of-error estimates, see `chg_*_moe` and the known issues before calling a small difference real.

**`geo_change` is measured per geoid, not assumed** — see the column's own description for the full definition of each value. At this geography: `identical` (92.9%, 52 of 56); `no_acs_data` (7.1%, 4). Never apportioned: the period a geoid's lineage does not support a legitimate figure for carries NULL on the affected columns rather than a modelled or carried-over number dressed up as a measurement.

Every estimate ships with its margin of error — `<column>_moe`, at the 90% confidence level, the level the Census Bureau publishes — and the headline statistics also carry a coefficient of variation (`<column>_cv` = `moe / 1.645 / estimate`) so small-area noise can be flagged rather than mapped as if certain. Derived percentages propagate error with the Census Bureau's own formulas from [ACS Accuracy of the Data](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/tech_docs/accuracy/MultiyearACSAccuracyofData2024.pdf): sums add in quadrature, proportions use the subset formula with the ratio formula as fallback. ACS jam values for suppressed or unestimable cells are NULL here, never numbers, and medians are published as the Bureau publishes them — a median cannot be aggregated to a larger area. Each column's description names its ACS table, its exact universe, and the lines summed to make it.

Geometries are the Census Bureau's [2024 cartographic boundary files](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/geo/cartographic-boundary.html) for states and state equivalents (GENZ2024, 1:500,000), reprojected to EPSG:4326 and joined on `GEOID`. Cartographic rather than TIGER/Line because TIGER extends census geographies into open water while the cartographic files clip to the shoreline, which is what a choropleth wants. The boundary vintage matches the estimate vintage.

Three collections, one per geography: [Trends, 2015–2024 — Counties](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/trends/county-trends/collection.json) and [Trends, 2015–2024 — Census Tracts](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/trends/tract-trends/collection.json). All three carry the same dozen indicators and the same `geo_change` taxonomy; they do not join to each other on `geoid` (a tract is not a county). This group sits beside the ACS themed chunks at the same geographies, not inside them — a different shape (long form, two periods, `geo_change`) from every themed collection this catalog otherwise publishes, and it carries only 12 of the catalog's ~135 indicators rather than a theme's full set. For every other 2020-2024 indicator at this geography, or for a 2020-2024-only question with no time dimension, see [states](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/states/catalog.json).

The PMTiles archive carries the same columns as the GeoParquet minus the `*_moe` columns, at every zoom, for the `2020-2024` period's features only (one geometry per geoid, with its `chg_*` columns already on it — tiling both periods' identical geometries twice would double the archive for no visual gain). Built with [tylertoo](https://github.com/geoparquet-io/tylertoo). Statistics come from the GeoParquet, which keeps both periods.

This is a mirror — [data.census.gov](https://data.census.gov/) is authoritative, and nothing here is an official Census Bureau product. The exact upstream files this collection was built from are published as `source` assets, linked at their original census.gov URLs, so the original is always one click away; the Bureau's own documentation is linked alongside them. Read the collection's [AGENTS.md](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/trends/state-trends/AGENTS.md) before querying: it carries the schema, the join keys, the NULL-rate story and tested queries against the published URLs.

## Schema

| geoid | string | 2-character state FIPS code, e.g. `29` for Missouri. Covers the 50 states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico and the four island areas. Matches `GEOID` in the 2024 cartographic boundary state file; the ACS `0400000US` prefix is stripped. Keyed to the geography the 2020-2024 ACS release tabulates (the same geoid every other collection at this geography uses); a 2015-2019 predecessor that no longer exists under this geoid (Connecticut's 8 old counties, Alaska's Valdez-Cordova Census Area) is not published as a row of its own -- see `geo_change`. |

| state | string | Two-letter USPS state or territory code (`MO`, `PR`, `GU`). Carried on every collection from the `STATEFP → USPS` lookup embedded in `tools/specs.py` rather than read off the boundary files, because the 2024 cartographic boundary block-group file has no `STUSPS` column while the tract, county and state files do. |
| period | string | Which ACS 5-year release this row's estimates come from: `2015-2019` (`acsdt5y2019`, the Census Bureau's table-based prototype summary file, validated against the Census API with 0 mismatches across ~4.66 million compared values before this collection was built) or `2020-2024` (`acsdt5y2024`, the same release every other ACS collection in this catalog reads). The two periods do not overlap, which is what makes them comparable in the first place -- see the collection's known_issues and [Comparing ACS Data](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/guidance/comparing-acs-data.html). |
| geo_change | string | How this row's geoid relates across the two periods -- measured against the real geometry change between the 2015-2019 and 2020-2024 vintages (population-weighted at tract level, via catalog/reference/crosswalks/t10t20-tract.parquet and catalog/blocks' 2020 population; exact at county level, via the preserved 6-digit tract number for Connecticut's 2022 county realignment), never assumed. One of five values:

- `identical` -- bidirectionally 1:1: this geoid's predecessor sent essentially all (>=99%) of its population here, and this geoid received essentially all of its population from that one predecessor. All 12 indicators carry over.
- `absorbed` -- several clean predecessors (each sending >=99% of ITS OWN population here) combine into this geoid. Counts (`population`, `households`) SUM across the predecessors' raw census cells with margins added in quadrature -- a legitimate aggregation -- and the five rate columns (`pct_below_poverty`, `pct_unemployed`, `pct_bachelors_plus`, `pct_people_of_color`, `pct_owner_occupied`) recompute from the summed numerator and denominator. The five medians (`median_hh_income`, `per_capita_income`, `median_age`, `median_home_value`, `median_gross_rent`) are NULL for the 2015-2019 period: a median cannot be derived from medians, so there is no legitimate figure for a geography the Census Bureau never tabulated directly.
- `subdivided` -- this geoid gets >=99% of its population from a single predecessor, but that predecessor itself sends population to more than one place (it is not a clean 1:1 match on its own side). The five rates and five medians carry over from that one predecessor under an explicit part-resembles-whole assumption -- flagged here, not hidden -- while `population` and `households` are NULL: carrying a whole predecessor's population onto a fragment of it would overstate the fragment.
- `mixed` -- genuinely mixed sources: no predecessor dominates and no clean set of feeders combines cleanly either. Every indicator is NULL for the 2015-2019 period.
- `no_acs_data` -- a cartographic boundary exists and the ACS 5-year program tabulates NEITHER period here (American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, the U.S. Virgin Islands). Not a geography change -- a program gap in both periods.

The period with no legitimate figure carries NULL, never an apportioned or carried-over number dressed up as a measurement. See the collection's known_issues for the measured share of each category and the Connecticut/Alaska specifics. |
| population | int64 | Total population. Universe: total population. The single line of [B01003](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B01003) (Total Population), published as an integer. ## Processing Notes

Built 2026-11-16 from `tools/build_trends.py`, reading B01002, B01003, B03002, B15003, B19013, B19301, B23025, B25003, B25064, B25077, C17002 from both the ACS 2015-2019 prototype summary file (https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2019/prototype/5YRData/) and the 2020-2024 production summary file (https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2024/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/), both over plain HTTPS, no API key for the bulk files.

**Re-pinned, not assumed.** Every line number this catalog's `tools/specs.py` uses was re-checked against the 2019 prototype's own table shells before any 2019 value was read: all 55 shell pins and all 120 (table, line) pairs resolve to the SAME line with the SAME concept in both vintages. Two label changes only, neither a line move: B05002 line 13 reads "Foreign born:" in 2019 versus "Foreign-born:" in 2024 (a hyphen, normalized away rather than hardcoded), and B08301 line 10 drops a parenthetical in 2024 that the pin's substring match already tolerated.

**Proven before anything depended on it.** The 2019 source is labelled a prototype, so it was validated against the Census API (`api.census.gov/data/2019/acs/acs5`) before this collection was built: 29 requests, stratified across state, county, 10 sampled states' full tract and block-group coverage (including Connecticut, Alaska and Puerto Rico), place, congressional district and a full national ZCTA pull — ~4.66 million raw cell values compared, 0 mismatches.

`geo_change` is measured the same way at every geography: population-weighted correspondence between the 2015-2019-vintage predecessor geography and the 2020-2024-vintage geography this catalog otherwise publishes, classified `identical` / `absorbed` / `subdivided` / `mixed` at a 99% population-share threshold each way (see the `geo_change` column description for the full rule). At county and state this needed real crosswalking in exactly two places, both measured by diffing the full set of county geoids each vintage's B01003 file carries: Connecticut's 8 old counties (2015-2019) versus 9 planning regions (2020-2024, effective 2022, mapped on the preserved 6-digit tract number against `catalog/blocks`' 2020 population — exact, not estimated, since whole tracts move together at this level) and Alaska's Valdez-Cordova Census Area splitting into Chugach and Copper River (population-weighted, via the tract crosswalk below, since this is a genuine 2010-to-2020 boundary change the Census Bureau's own TIGER vintages already carry).

`chg_<indicator>` is the 2020-2024 value minus the 2015-2019 value, computed once both periods' rows exist and carried on the 2020-2024 row only; `chg_<indicator>_moe` is the quadrature sum of the two periods' margins (ACS Accuracy of the Data's formula for the margin of a difference of two independent estimates).

Geometry is the same 2024 cartographic boundary file (cb_2024_us_state_500k.zip) the 2020-2024 themed ACS collections at this geography join to, reused rather than re-fetched, for both periods' rows.

Converted to GeoParquet with geoparquet-io (gpio): ZSTD compression, Hilbert row order, and a covering `bbox` column with row-group statistics, so a spatial filter can skip most of the file over the network. Tiled to PMTiles with tylertoo as a census-geography pyramid, so coarse zooms carry a coarser census geography — counties, then tracts — with that geography's own official values, instead of a thinned sample of this collection's features; the collections small enough to draw nationally (counties, states, congressional districts) are tiled as real polygons at every zoom.

## Known Issues

**Never apportioned.** Where `geo_change` says a period has no legitimate figure for a geoid (`mixed` always; `subdivided` for `population`/`households`; `absorbed` for the five medians; `no_acs_data` always), that cell is NULL. No value here was ever modelled, interpolated or carried over from a different geography and published as if it were a direct measurement.

**A median cannot be derived from medians.** `absorbed` rows (several 2015-2019 predecessors combine into one 2020-2024 geoid) NULL `median_hh_income`, `per_capita_income`, `median_age`, `median_home_value` and `median_gross_rent` for 2015-2019 for exactly this reason — summing the predecessors' raw census cells and recomputing is legitimate for counts and rates, and this build does it, but there is no published 2015-2019 median for a geography the Census Bureau never tabulated directly, so inventing one by averaging or reweighting predecessor medians would be a model wearing a measurement's clothes.

**`subdivided` carries an explicit assumption.** A `subdivided` geoid's rates and medians are its single dominant 2015-2019 predecessor's own published values, copied over under a part-resembles-whole assumption — the predecessor's overall rate or median is assumed to hold for the fragment too, which is not measured and can be wrong for a fragment that differs from its parent's average. `population` and `households` are NULL there instead of carrying the whole predecessor's count, which would overstate a fragment.

**Measured `geo_change` distribution at this geography:** `identical` 52 (92.9%), `no_acs_data` 4 (7.1%) of 56 geoids.

**The four dollar medians are not on a common dollar basis across the two periods, and `chg_median_hh_income`, `chg_per_capita_income`, `chg_median_home_value` and `chg_median_gross_rent` inherit that.** Each ACS release reports its dollar medians in ITS OWN vintage's inflation-adjusted dollars — `B19013` is titled "...in 2019 inflation-adjusted dollars" in the 2015-2019 release and "...in 2024 inflation-adjusted dollars" in the 2020-2024 release, exactly as published, not re-based to a common dollar-year by this build. A positive `chg_median_hh_income` mixes real change with five years of inflation, and it shows: measured at state level, 52 of 52 states show a nominal increase and 0 show a decrease, which is implausible as a real-income statement over five years for every single state and is this build's own evidence that the comparison is partly a dollar-basis effect, not a finding that incomes rose everywhere. This is why the collection's diverging style classifies on `chg_pct_below_poverty` (a percentage-point difference, immune to this) rather than on a dollar column. Deflate `median_hh_income`/`per_capita_income`/`median_home_value`/`median_gross_rent` to a common dollar-year yourself (e.g. CPI-U-RS) before treating any `chg_<dollar column>` as a real-dollar change.

**The two periods do not overlap, which is what makes them comparable.** 2015-2019 and 2020-2024 share no sample year, unlike two 5-year releases one year apart (2019-2023 and 2020-2024, say), which the Census Bureau's own guidance on [Comparing ACS Data](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/guidance/comparing-acs-data.html) says not to compare because their overlapping sample years make a measured difference partly an artifact of shared data. This collection's two periods are five years apart with no shared sample year, so a difference here is a measured change, not a sampling artifact of the comparison itself — though it is still a difference of two margin-of-error estimates, see `chg_*_moe` and the known issues before calling a small difference real.

This group carries 12 of the catalog's ~135 ACS indicators. For every other indicator, or for a 2020-2024-only question with no time dimension, use the current-vintage themed collections at this geography instead — they are not a finer or coarser version of these numbers, they are the same survey at the same geography with the rest of the columns.
