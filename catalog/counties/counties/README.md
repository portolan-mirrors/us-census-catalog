# All Indicators — Counties

**[🗺️ Open in the data browser](https://browser.portolan-sdi.org/?catalog=https://data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/catalog.json#/external/data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/counties/collection.json)** · [Download the GeoParquet](https://data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/counties.parquet) · [Agent guide](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/counties/AGENTS.md) · [Source repository](https://github.com/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog)

Every indicator in this catalog's ACS column set, in one table: income and poverty, age and race and education, housing cost and tenure, vehicles and commuting and internet access, health insurance and disability and nativity, and median household income by race and ethnicity. At this many features width costs little, so the whole 135-column table is published here as well as a theme at a time in the five themed chunks beside it — a cross-theme question needs no join.

Curated indicators for all **3,235** counties and county equivalents in the United States, Puerto Rico and the island areas, from the U.S. Census Bureau's [American Community Survey](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs.html) **2020–2024 5-year estimates** (`acsdt5y2024`, the table-based [Summary File](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2024/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/)). The Census Bureau calls the ACS "the premier source of detailed information about the nation's people and housing". The Bureau also publishes 1-year estimates at this geography; the 5-year release is used throughout this catalog so every geography in it is the same vintage and directly comparable.

Every estimate ships with its margin of error — `<column>_moe`, at the 90% confidence level, the level the Census Bureau publishes — and the headline statistics also carry a coefficient of variation (`<column>_cv` = `moe / 1.645 / estimate`) so small-area noise can be flagged rather than mapped as if certain. Derived percentages propagate error with the Census Bureau's own formulas from [ACS Accuracy of the Data](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/tech_docs/accuracy/MultiyearACSAccuracyofData2024.pdf): sums add in quadrature, proportions use the subset formula with the ratio formula as fallback. ACS jam values for suppressed or unestimable cells are NULL here, never numbers, and medians are published as the Bureau publishes them — a median cannot be aggregated to a larger area. Each column's description names its ACS table, its exact universe, and the lines summed to make it.

Counties are the coarsest geography most questions are actually asked at, and the most reliable in this catalog below state level: the median coefficient of variation on `median_hh_income` is **0.042** here against 0.125 at tract level (measured). One context column breaks the catalog's own rule — `population_moe` is NULL on **3,103 of the 3,235 rows**, only 132 small rural counties carrying a margin on `population` — so read `population` here as a count to use rather than an estimate to put an interval on.

Geometries are the Census Bureau's [2024 cartographic boundary files](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/geo/cartographic-boundary.html) for counties and county equivalents (GENZ2024, 1:500,000), reprojected to EPSG:4326 and joined on `GEOID`. Cartographic rather than TIGER/Line because TIGER extends census geographies into open water while the cartographic files clip to the shoreline, which is what a choropleth wants. The boundary vintage matches the estimate vintage.

The join is driven by geometry, so a geography with 2024 boundaries but no ACS tabulation keeps its row with NULL values instead of disappearing: 13 county equivalents in the island areas, which have cartographic boundaries but no ACS 5-year estimates because the Island Areas run a separate decennial-only program. Keeping these rows means a national map has no holes and a consumer can see the absence; dropping them would make "every US geography" quietly mean something narrower.

**This is the full table** — all 135 columns on every row, the file to read when a question crosses themes or when you want one row with everything on it. The same rows are also published as five themed chunks of 29–46 columns each — [Income & Poverty](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/county-income/collection.json), [People & Education](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/county-people/collection.json), [Housing & Rent](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/county-housing/collection.json), [Commuting & Connectivity](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/county-connectivity/collection.json) and [Health, Disability & Income by Race](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/county-wellbeing/collection.json) — for a consumer who wants one theme and not the rest; each carries the same `geoid`, the same geometry and the same seven context columns, so it joins 1:1 back to this table. This collection's metadata sits beside those chunks, but its GeoParquet and PMTiles stay one directory up at `counties/counties.parquet` and `counties/counties.pmtiles`, which is where they have always been. GEOIDs nest by prefix: `substr(geoid, 1, 2)` is the state, and tracts and block groups join back on their own first 5 characters. Every other geography in this catalog is organized the same way — [block groups](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/block-groups/catalog.json), [census tracts](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/tracts/catalog.json), [states](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/states/catalog.json), [ZCTAs](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/zctas/catalog.json), [places and CDPs](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/places/catalog.json) and [congressional districts](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/congressional-districts/catalog.json) — and the [blocks](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/blocks/collection.json) collection carries 2020 Decennial counts, a different survey and vintage rather than a finer version of these numbers.

The PMTiles archive carries the same columns as the GeoParquet, minus the `*_moe` columns, and at every zoom the tiles are the real census geographies — there are few enough features here to draw them all at a national zoom, so this archive needs none of the coarser-geography coarse-zoom bands the catalog's finer collections carry. Built with [tylertoo](https://github.com/geoparquet-io/tylertoo). Statistics still come from the GeoParquet: margins of error are not in the tiles.

This is a mirror — [data.census.gov](https://data.census.gov/) is authoritative, and nothing here is an official Census Bureau product. The exact upstream files this collection was built from are published as `source` assets, linked at their original census.gov URLs, so the original is always one click away; the Bureau's own documentation is linked alongside them. Read the collection's [AGENTS.md](https://source.coop/portolan-mirrors/us-census-catalog/counties/counties/AGENTS.md) before querying: it carries the schema, the join keys, the NULL-rate story and tested queries against the published URLs.

## Processing Notes



Built 2026-11-12 from the ACS 2020–2024 5-year table-based Summary File (https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2024/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/), downloaded the same day over plain HTTPS — the summary file needs no API key. Tables read for this collection: B01001, B01002, B01003, B03002, B05002, B08301, B08303, B15003, B17001, B17020B, B18101, B19001, B19013, B19013B, B19013D, B19013H, B19013I, B19083, B19301, B20002, B22010, B23025, B25002, B25003, B25024, B25034, B25044, B25064, B25070, B25077, B26001, B27001, B28001, B28002, C17002. Each national `.dat` file is one row per geography with estimate and margin of error side by side, and every summary level is picked off `GEO_ID` by prefix in a single pass per file — this collection's is `0500000US`, read off the files themselves rather than taken from a summary-level table.

Jam values (the sentinels the ACS publishes in place of unestimable values, e.g. -666666666 for an uncomputable median, and -555555555 for a controlled MOE) become NULL: any estimate or margin at or below -111111111 is dropped rather than published as a number. Counts are sums of the specified lines with margins added in quadrature; percentages use the Census Bureau's proportion formula with the ratio formula as fallback; medians are passed through untouched; coefficients of variation are `moe / 1.645 / estimate` on the headline columns. Every line number is pinned against the release's own table shells before the build starts (44 pins verified, 0 failures, plus 4 derived line groups resolved from the shells), so a vintage bump that moves a line fails loudly instead of publishing a mislabeled column.

Geometry is the 2024 cartographic boundary file (cb_2024_us_county_500k.zip) for counties and county equivalents (1:500,000), reprojected to EPSG:4326 with ogr2ogr — not DuckDB's `ST_Transform`, which computes correct coordinates but stamps the source CRS into the GeoParquet metadata. Geometry drives the join, so a geography with boundaries but no ACS tabulation keeps its row with NULL values.

Cross-checked end to end against the published St. Louis census catalog, which runs the same formulas on the same vintage: 314 of 314 block groups joined, 0 mismatches on median household income, its margin of error, the poverty rate and its margin of error — exact equality, not approximate.



Converted to GeoParquet with geoparquet-io (gpio): ZSTD compression, Hilbert row order, and a covering `bbox` column with row-group statistics, so a spatial filter can skip most of the file over the network. Tiled to PMTiles with tylertoo as a census-geography pyramid, so coarse zooms carry a coarser census geography — counties, then tracts — with that geography's own official values, instead of a thinned sample of this collection's features; the collections small enough to draw nationally (counties, states, congressional districts) are tiled as real polygons at every zoom.

## Known Issues

Island areas carry geometry but no ACS data. American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and the U.S. Virgin Islands have cartographic boundaries but no ACS 5-year estimates, so their rows publish with NULL values rather than being dropped: 4 states, 13 county equivalents, 126 tracts, 405 block groups, 17 ZCTAs, 279 places and 4 delegate districts.

**A family of margins is NULL at this geography, and `population_moe` is only the visible one.** Measured: `population_moe`, `pct_under_18_moe`, `pct_65_plus_moe`, `pct_white_nh_moe`, `pct_black_moe`, `pct_native_moe`, `pct_asian_moe`, `pct_hispanic_moe`, `pct_people_of_color_moe` and `pct_foreign_born_moe` are carried on 132 of the 3,235 rows. The 132 that carry one are the smallest counties in the country: every one has a population under 7,000 (measured). `sqrt(sum(moe*moe))` over a NULL-bearing column returns NULL, so check before putting one in a quadrature sum, and read these estimates as something to use rather than as an estimate to put an interval on. Every other `*_moe` column in this collection is populated on every row where its estimate is, except `pct_bachelors_plus` on 99 rows, `median_hh_income_black` on 22 rows, `median_hh_income_asian` on 42 rows, `median_hh_income_hispanic` on 10 rows (measured).

That NULL is upstream, and it is not suppression: each column in the list above comes from a total-population table (B01001, B01003, B03002, B05002), and in the release's own `.dat` files those rows carry the estimate beside `-555555555`, the **controlled-MOE** jam value a controlled estimate gets in place of a sampling margin. The build turns it into NULL rather than publishing it as a number — `0500000US01001|59947|-555555555` in `acsdt5y2024-b01003.dat`, read 2026-11-15. The same columns carry margins normally at the finer geographies, where the estimates are not controlled this way: `population_moe` is carried on 33,759 of 33,791 ZCTAs and on all 241,854 populated block groups (measured).

Medians are published for nearly every county; the race iterations are the exception: NULL for `median_hh_income` on 0.43% of rows, `median_gross_rent` on 0.62% of rows, `median_home_value` on 0.56% of rows, `median_hh_income_white_nh` on 2.38% of rows, `median_hh_income_hispanic` on 19.60% of rows, `median_hh_income_black` on 39.47% of rows, `median_hh_income_asian` on 53.63% of rows (measured). These are the Census Bureau's own suppressions, reported rather than patched. A map of a race-iteration median is as much a map of where that group lives as of its income, and quoting a racial income gap without its denominator overstates the coverage.

Margins of error at this geography can be large relative to the estimate. Use the `*_cv` columns to flag or grey out unreliable values; never compare two geographies without checking whether their margins overlap, and never aggregate a median.

The PMTiles archive is real geographies at every zoom, with no coarser-geography coarse-zoom band (the catalog's finer collections switch to counties and tracts at coarse zooms; this one has few enough features not to). `*_moe` columns are not in the tiles — statistics come from the GeoParquet.
